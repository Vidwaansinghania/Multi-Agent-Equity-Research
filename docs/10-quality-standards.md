# 10 · Quality standards

The bar is sell-side equity research and a CFA Institute Research Challenge
submission. Every prompt in [docs/04-role-prompts.md](04-role-prompts.md) points
here, and a report that would not pass the checklist at the bottom is a failed run.

## Language

Write confident, direct statements. "We expect the installed base to reach 2 billion
units by FY2028," not "the installed base might possibly reach." Give specific
figures with dates: "FY2026E revenue of $8,820m," not "revenue is expected to
increase." State causal logic: "Supply constraints held volume, and we expect them
to clear by Q3."

Professional qualifiers where a claim is genuinely an estimate: we estimate, we
believe, we expect, in our view. Not vague hedging: it appears that, it seems like,
one might think. Not excessive caveats stacked on one sentence.

No emotional language and no personal opinions unrelated to valuation. No unsourced
assertion of quality, "management is strong" being the standard example. No repeating
the same point across sections.

## Sourcing

Primary sources first: 10-K, 10-Q and 8-K, cited by filing and date; earnings call
transcripts by quarter and date; investor presentations; published management
guidance. Secondary where needed: consensus with the date it was pulled and the
source named, industry reports by title and date, peer filings for comparisons.

Cite inline: "Revenue grew 5.3% to $8,400m (FY2025 10-K), on volume rather than
price." Then list sources as a numbered appendix in the order they appear.

An unsourced number is treated as absent by the agent downstream, so an uncited
figure is wasted work.

## Metrics and units

Define every non-GAAP measure once, at first use. Specify currency on every figure
and keep units consistent within a series. Note the exchange rate where a figure was
converted.

Use fiscal-year notation with suffixes: FY2025A for actual, FY2026E for estimate.
State the fiscal year end once, early. Label forward and trailing multiples as such.
Flag estimates as [est.] and consensus as [consensus].

## Ratings

Three tiers on the probability-weighted twelve-month total return: BUY above +15%,
HOLD between −15% and +15%, SELL below −15%. Map the number onto the band and take
the answer.

No fourth tier, no paired labels, and nothing that means "HOLD but I like it."
Practical instructions like "trim" belong in the entry-discipline table, not in the
rating. Where the call sits within five points of a band edge, say how marginal it is
and name the single assumption that would move it.

Quality and price are separate questions. State the call and the entry discipline
separately.

## Valuation

Three scenarios with explicit probabilities summing to 1, each with its own earnings
path, its own multiple, and a sentence defending its weight. A probability-weighted
target, and the total return that implies from the reference price including
dividends.

Methodology stated rather than implied: the discounting convention, the terminal
method, how net debt was computed, how leases and share-based compensation were
treated. Terminal value reported as a share of enterprise value. A reverse discounted
cash flow as a check on what the current price already assumes.

A sensitivity grid on the two assumptions that move the target most, showing the
target at several values of each.

## Risks

Exactly five, each material and specific, each with the potential impact as a target
price and a percentage, a probability of low, moderate or high, what would have to
happen, and the mitigation.

"Macro risk" is not a risk. "A 100bp rise in the thirty-year mortgage rate holds
existing home sales below 4M SAAR, cutting comparable sales to −2% and the target to
$X" is.

## Disclosures

Four blocks, in this order: the rating scale with its bands, the analyst
certification, ownership and conflicts stated as fact from the holdings snapshot, and
an educational-and-research-only disclaimer.

Ownership is a fact, not a caveat. State the position: company, share count, share of
portfolio, snapshot date. Never write commentary about whether holding the stock
biases the call.

## Checklist before publication

Research

- [ ] Every claim sourced, with no unsourced assertions
- [ ] Financial data from filings or named consensus, never estimated silently
- [ ] Bull, base and bear internally consistent, probabilities summing to 1
- [ ] Peer comparison justifies the valuation against peers
- [ ] Sensitivity grid shows which assumptions matter
- [ ] Ten fiscal years of statements, or a stated reason for fewer
- [ ] Porter's five forces table with an evidence column, not adjectives
- [ ] Football field with all five ranges against the reference price
- [ ] Dated catalyst calendar, confirmed dates marked apart from estimated

Writing

- [ ] Rating, target and horizon stated upfront
- [ ] No vague language: might, could, possibly
- [ ] Consistent terminology, currency and units
- [ ] Five specific risks in the required format
- [ ] The thesis statable in two or three sentences

Structure

- [ ] Sections numbered and in order, each readable on its own
- [ ] Exhibits titled with their source
- [ ] Sources numbered in the appendix
- [ ] Twelve to eighteen pages when set

Final read

- [ ] Can a busy portfolio manager get the thesis in five minutes
- [ ] Are the drivers of the valuation identified explicitly
- [ ] Would you stake your credibility on the recommendation
