# Fundamentals — ACME

Every figure below is invented. See `run.md`.

## What the business earns

Ten fiscal years, FY2016 to FY2025, are in `statements.csv`. Revenue grew from
$5,200m to $8,400m, a 5.5% compound rate, with one down year in FY2020 when
industrial production fell. Operating margin widened from 16.1% to 19.3% across
the decade, and the widening is real rather than mix: gross margin rose 1.8
points while SG&A held at roughly 17% of revenue.

Two segments. Industrial coatings is 65.6% of FY2025 revenue and grew 5.9% a
year over the decade. Specialty adhesives is the rest and grew 5.1%, with the
faster margin improvement of the two.

## Quality of earnings

Free cash flow conversion averaged 108% of net income over ten years and never
fell below 92%, which is the strongest single number in the file. Depreciation
has tracked capital expenditure within 15% every year, so the margin expansion
is not deferred maintenance. Share-based compensation ran $58m in FY2025, 0.7%
of revenue, and is charged in the figures above.

Two accounting choices matter. Development costs on new adhesive formulations
are expensed as incurred, which understates reported margin against peers who
capitalise. And the coatings segment recognises revenue on shipment, so a
quarter-end push shows up in receivables rather than in cash.

## Returns on capital

Return on invested capital was 15.1% on average and 16.4% in FY2025. The
incremental return matters more: capital deployed in FY2025 was $430m of capital
expenditure plus $95m of working capital, against a $107.8m increase in
after-tax operating profit, which is a 20.5% incremental return. New money is
earning more than the historical base, which is the opposite of the usual
pattern in industrial chemicals.

## Balance sheet

Net debt of $1,800m is 0.91x EBITDA, down from 1.86x in FY2016. The maturity
schedule has no wall: $340m due FY2027, $520m FY2029, the rest after FY2031.
Free cash flow of $1,340m covered $292m of dividends and $600m of buybacks with
$448m left over.
