# Bull case — ACME

Every figure below is invented. See `run.md`.

The base case is too conservative in three places, and two of them are testable
inside twelve months.

## The 19.6% operating margin is a floor, not a forecast

The valuation memo holds margin at 19.6% for ten years. The fundamentals file
shows margin rising 320bp over the past decade with no year of reversal outside
FY2020, and the incremental return on capital in FY2025 was 20.5% against a
15.1% historical average. A business earning more on new money than on old money
does not hold margin flat for a decade. At 21.0% by FY2029, which is the trend
line rather than an extrapolation of the best year, free cash flow in FY2035 is
$2,290m instead of $2,115m and the value per share is $113.40.

## The Dalton ramp is a dated catalyst, not a hope

The October 8-K attributed part of the guidance cut to the delayed adhesives
ramp, and the news file dates full utilisation to the Q2 fiscal 2026 print on
2026-07-29. That is a specific event inside the horizon. Adhesives is the higher
margin segment and the plant is already built and paid for, so the capital is
sunk and only the volume is missing.

## The bear probability is too high for a risk the customers have not acted on

Two coatings customers said they are evaluating insourcing. Evaluating is not
building, a coatings line costs $180m and takes two years to permit on the
industry analyst's own numbers, and the customers in question are buying roughly
$600m a year, so the make-versus-buy arithmetic is not obviously in their favour.
A 15% bear probability with 35% on base is the honest split, and on those weights
the target is $117.15 rather than $108.50.

## What would change my mind

A Q1 fiscal 2026 print on 2026-04-23 showing coatings volume down year on year,
or either named customer disclosing a capital commitment to a coatings line. The
first is a number I can check in April; the second would be an 8-K.
